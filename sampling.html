Sampling Protocols

A sampling protocol that is wrong does not fail loudly. It quietly misprices ore, breaks reconciliation and corrupts resource estimates. This work is laboratory and data based: I design and audit protocols under the Theory of Sampling, and analyse the numbers your laboratory and drilling campaign already produce.

  • Protocol audit (Theory of Sampling)

    I review your documented protocol and preparation chain stage by stage against Gy’s theory — delimitation, extraction, preparation and weighting errors — and quantify where variance is entering the result.

  • New protocol design

    Built from your material in the laboratory: heterogeneity testing, minimum sample mass at each stage, the crushing and splitting sequence, and the nomogram that justifies every decision.

  • Drilling QA/QC programmes

    Duplicate, standard and blank programmes sized for your campaign, with control charts and laboratory performance analysis that stand up to external audit and resource reporting requirements.

  • Sampling error in reconciliation

    When the block model, the mine and the plant disagree, I quantify how much of the gap comes from sampling and preparation error — the component most reviews never measure.

Where this pays for itself

Sampling error stays invisible until it becomes expensive. A grade bias of a fraction of a percent changes the price of a shipment, the cut-off grade applied to a pit, and the confidence of a resource statement. Correcting the protocol costs a fraction of a single mispriced consignment.
